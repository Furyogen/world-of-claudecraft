## Summary

Adds two rideable mounts built from the rift push-puzzle rock: the boulder
players shove onto a socket to open the next floor, now saddled and ridden in
both of its states.

### Pet Rock (common, +60%)

Deliberately the stablemaster horse's card exactly: `common`, `moveSpeedPct: 0.6`.
The joke should cost nothing in power, so it is the Valorsteed's numbers wearing
a rock rather than a new balance point.

It reuses `public/models/props/rock_large_f.glb`, the same craggy mesh
`door_portal.ts` already dresses as `rift_boulder`, so the mount and the puzzle
rock are recognisably one object. That GLB carries no animation track at all,
which makes this a clipless prop-lane mount like the snail and hover cycle: it
joins `CLIPLESS_RIGS`, and its hop bob **is** its locomotion, grinding along
while moving and sitting dead still when stopped.

### Shiny Pet Rock (epic, +80%)

The same rock after it has been socketed and the rift has lit it, riding the
epic tier's standard 80%. The gag is that the only difference between the common
and the epic is that this one was finally put where it belongs, so it is the
same mesh in the placed palette (`0x8a7550` stone under `0xffc24a` veins, lifted
directly from `rift_boulder_placed`) rather than a second asset.

The visual tell is motion, not just colour: the common hops at 1.6 Hz only while
moving, the socketed one hovers at 0.9 Hz and idles in place, which is what
`tests/mount_visuals.test.ts` pins.

### Ambient effects (grit, riftglow)

Both mounts previously had `fx: null`, so a mount whose whole description is
that it grinds along showed nothing at all when moving. This adds two effects:

- **grit** for the common: gravel thrown behind the rock, rate ramping with
  speed and clamped at full grind, and **zero while stationary** (a stationary
  dust cloud reads as a bug).
- **riftglow** for the epic: gold motes rising off the crown in a ring, on at
  idle too because that mount hovers rather than rests, denser on the move. It
  rides `hdr()` like every other emissive emitter, so it blooms on tiers with a
  composer and stays as authored on those without.

The emission math is a new pure module, `src/render/mount_fx_core.ts`, with its
randomness injected so a Vitest pins exact motes. Following the review outcome
on #3365 for this same seam, the kind-to-emitter mapping is **not** an if/else
chain in `renderer.ts`: it lives in `src/render/mount_fx.ts` behind an
exhaustive switch with a `never` default, so a future `fx` kind is a compile
error rather than a mount that silently renders nothing.

### Acquisition: none yet, deliberately

Neither mount has a live acquisition path (owner call). Both ship as complete
catalog and visual entries the way `drakemaw_raptor` already does, and both are
listed explicitly in the Reliquary's source-pending ledger so a sourceless mount
is a recorded decision rather than an accident. `tests/mounts.test.ts` proves it
means no path: not on any mob table, not heroic-reachable, and in none of the
three rift reins pools.

### Item art: a recorded gap, not a claimed pass

Both reins icons are vector-authored originals. They pass every machine check
(opaque, 128x128, in budget, unique hash) and were reviewed for subject identity
and small-size readability at 128/40/28/22, 28-grayscale, and the 64px circular
crop. They do **not** fully satisfy the `woc-item-icon-v1` style contract:
against the painted `reins_*` family they read flat.

That is recorded in the audit's `incrementalReviews` rather than hidden behind a
pass, on the same footing as the `reins_grimtusk_boar` gap in #3365.
**Repainting is a scoped follow-up and a reasonable thing to ask for before
merge.** Every derived digest (shipping catalog, sheet set, catalog evidence)
was regenerated by `scripts/item_art_audit.mjs`, not hand-set.

  `tests/item_icons.test.ts`, `tests/item_art_consistency.test.ts`,
  `tests/item_art_audit_builder.test.ts`, `tests/sfx.test.ts`,
  `tests/sfx_manifest.test.ts`, `tests/chat.test.ts`, `tests/guide.test.ts`,
  `tests/architecture.test.ts`, `tests/localization_fixes.test.ts`,
  `tests/i18n_completeness.test.ts`, `tests/deeds_content.test.ts`,
  `tests/eastbrook_polish_capture_contract.test.ts`,
  `tests/eastbrook_polish_artifact_integrity.test.ts`.
- Manual steps: booted the offline client, granted both reins and
  `ridingTrained` through the dev sim handle, and rode each mount. Confirmed the
  live speed multipliers (1.6 and 1.8), that the rider seats on the crown at
  both sizes, that the common's grit fires only while moving and the epic's glow
  persists at idle, and that each mount plays its own stride clip.
- **Honest note on the local suite:** this Windows machine fails 34 suites on a
  pristine `release/v0.38.0` checkout (symlink cases needing Developer Mode,
  plus `EPERM` temp-dir cleanup). I ran the full suite on both this branch and a
  clean base worktree and diffed the failing sets: **the branch-only set is
  empty**, so every remaining local failure is pre-existing and environmental.
  Linux CI is the real gate.
