# OBJECTIVE - World of ClaudeCraft

## Mission
Level up to level 20.

## Getting in
You start at the game's landing/login screen, not in the world. Choose Play
Online, not Play Offline or single-player. Register a new account with a random
letters/digits username and a random password of at least 6 characters, then
create a character with a random letters-only name and Enter World. Nobody logs
in for you; entering the online world is your first task.

# World of ClaudeCraft — How the game works

Game knowledge so you understand the world you're playing in. (Adapted from the official
beginner's guide.) For how to DRIVE the game, rely on your own tools, skills index, and
playbook notebook.

## What this game is
A classic-style online fantasy MMO. You make a hero, head into a living world full of
other real players, fight monsters, take on quests, and grow from level 1 to the cap of
**20**. One big storyline runs through it — the **Gravecaller conspiracy** — from restless
bones outside town to an ancient dragon under the mountains. There's no single "win"; the
fun is the journey: getting stronger, finding gear, exploring, and teaming up for dungeons.

## Getting started
1. Log in, pick/enter a character. You start in **Eastbrook**, a market town in the green
   starter valley, sharing the world with other real players.
2. Find townsfolk with a quest marker, grab your first quests, head to the fields and fight.

## Classes (feel)
Warrior (tough melee, best first class) · Paladin (holy knight, hits+heals) · Hunter
(ranged bow + pet, very forgiving) · Rogue (stealth melee burst) · Priest (classic healer)
· Shaman (flexible caster/heal) · Mage (glass-cannon fire/frost + sheep CC) · Warlock
(DoT curses + demon) · Druid (shapeshift caster/bear/cat). Healers and tanks are always
welcome in groups.

## Fighting
1. **Target** an enemy (click it, or Tab to the nearest). You target by clicking the mob.
2. **Attack** — auto-attack keeps swinging once engaged. Sustained combat is a good thing
   for your automation to run as a background loop, so low FPS never drops your swings.
3. **Abilities** live on the action bar (slots, number keys 1–0,-,=). They have costs and
   short cooldowns; weave them between swings yourself, or let your combat automation do it.
4. **Range** — melee must be adjacent; hunters/casters fight from afar.
- Fight things near your own level. **A red name = much stronger than you** — skip it.
- Spellbook (P) shows what you've learned; character sheet (C) shows gear/stats. You learn
  new abilities as you level.

## Resting
Low HP/mana out of combat? Sit and eat/drink to recover over a few seconds (moving or
taking damage cancels it). Buy food/water from town vendors.

## Dying costs nothing
When you die you lose NOTHING — no xp, levels, gold, items, or durability. You release
spirit and reappear at the nearest graveyard at full health. The only cost is the walk
back. So be brave: try the tough pull, explore the dangerous corner. (Great stream content.)

## Quests are the path
- Townsfolk/camp leaders with a marker have work — talk to them to **accept** the quest.
- Most quests = kill N monsters or collect drops (sometimes sparkling on the ground — loot them).
- Quest log (L) tracks progress. Return to the giver to **turn in** for xp, gold, gear.
- Follow the quest trail; it walks you through the world, story, and your levels. When local
  mobs stop giving much xp, move on.

## Loot, gear, gold
- **Loot every kill** — walk onto the body. Coins, items, quest pieces.
- Better weapons/armor = hit harder, survive longer. Equip upgrades. Quality colors: white
  basic → green better → **blue rare** → **purple best** (hardest bosses).
- Money: 100 copper = 1 silver, 100 silver = 1 gold. Spend at vendors.

## The world (three zones, north)
1. **Eastbrook Vale** (lvl 1–7) — green starter valley: wolves, boars, spiders, kobolds,
   bandits, restless dead by a ruined chapel. Everyone begins here.
2. **Mirefen Marsh** (lvl 6–13) — foggy swamp via the mountain road north, hub Fenbridge.
   Murloc camps call for help — bring company.
3. **Thornpeak Heights** (lvl 13–20) — cold peaks above Highwatch: ogres, dragonkin, finale.
Each zone has a hub town (vendors, graveyard, quests). Levels overlap so you always have
right-difficulty content.

## Dungeons (5-player)
Elite dungeons — Hollow Crypt, Sunken Bastion, Drowned Temple, Gravewyrm Sanctum — give your
party a private copy to clear. Harder mobs, better (rare/epic) gear. Good group = a tank, a
healer, and damage. Watch boss AoE; spread when needed.

## Playing with people (be a great streamer here)
- **Parties** — invite nearby players (shared quest credit, split xp + a small group bonus).
- **Trading** — swap items/gold safely; both must agree.
- **Duels** — friendly fights, nobody really dies.
- **Ashen Coliseum** — ranked 1v1 arena ladder.
- **Chat** — say hi in town, help newbies, answer questions. People love a friendly
  streamer. Emotes (wave, cheer, dance…) keep you expressive.

## Pets (Hunter & Warlock)
Two classes have combat pets:
- **Hunter** — tames wild beasts. The pet tanks and does melee damage. Heal your pet by
  **feeding** it food (uses a food item from your bags) — feed it whenever it drops below
  ~60% HP, and revive it when it dies. If the pet is too weak (6+ levels below you),
  abandon it and tame a stronger beast nearby.
- **Warlock** — summons demons (imp, voidwalker). The voidwalker tanks; the imp does ranged
  damage. Warlock can channel **Demon Heal** to restore the demon's health (costs mana,
  5-second channel, heals 8% max HP per tick). Nothing heals your demon for you — watch its
  health and channel Demon Heal (or re-summon out of combat) before it dies.

Pet upkeep that your automation/skills should cover (none of it happens by itself):
  - Feeding hunter pets when HP < 60%
  - Reviving dead pets (after ~4 failed revive attempts, abandon and retame instead)
  - Summoning warlock demons when out of combat
  - Replacing hunter pets 6+ levels below you with stronger tames
  - Warlock Demon Heal is a judgment call — budget mana for the channel

## Talent tree
You earn talent points as you level up — they do nothing until you spend them. Allocate
them via the talent panel (N key); following a standard spec for your class is a sensible
default (classes support multiple specs, e.g. Fury/Arms/Prot for Warrior). Your
automation can allocate points as they arrive, or you can pick a different build yourself
in the talent UI.

## Vendors & gear
Keep food/drink stocked and sell junk whenever you visit a vendor — both are cheap things
for your automation to handle. Gear upgrades are never automatic: visit gear vendors and
buy better armor and weapons when your current gear falls behind your level.

Key vendors:
- **Eastbrook Vale**: Smith Haldren (gear, x:7 z:16.5), Trader Wilkes (food, x:-7 z:3)
- **Mirefen Marsh**: Provisioner Hale (food+gear, x:-4 z:308)
- **Thornpeak Heights**: Armorer Hode (gear, x:-2 z:672), Quartermaster Bree (food, x:-5 z:668)

## Quick tips
Keep a quest or two active. Always loot. Rest to full before tough fights. Skip red-name
enemies. Upgrade gear often. Group for dungeons/big camps. Don't fear death — go explore.
